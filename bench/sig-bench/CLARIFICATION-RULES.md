# Clarification rules for the sig benchmark

Date checked for every row: 2026-11-13. Baseline jurisdiction: Florida.

Tiers:

- **LAW**: a statute or regulation requires the element, or limits what a pharmacist may change or assume.
- **STANDARD**: a professional or safety standard says to clarify (ISMP, USP, NABP, NCPDP, Joint Commission, non-binding board or agency guidance).
- **JUDGMENT**: no rule requires clarification. The pharmacist may interpret and document.

Labels used below: **Verified** means the text was read this session at the cited URL. **Inferred** means it is my reading of verified text, not a stated rule. **Not found** means I searched and found no source. This is research for a benchmark, not legal advice.

Source notes. Florida statutes were read on Online Sunshine (leg.state.fl.us, the Legislature's official site). Florida, California and New York rule text was read on the Cornell LII mirror, so the official flrules.org link is given beside each Florida rule. The eCFR site blocked automated reads, so 21 CFR 1306.05 was read on govinfo.gov (2024 annual edition). The ISMP high-alert lists were read from a 2021 copy hosted by a third party (scchc.org) because the ECRI page hides the PDF behind a download button. The NCPDP SCRIPT Implementation Recommendations (v1.77, August 2026) and the NABP Model Act (August 2026) were downloaded from their publishers' own sites without a login. The USP chapter text was read on a non-official mirror of USP 38 because the official text is subscription-only (see the USP row).

## Q&A

**1. Must a Florida prescription include directions for use? Is "as directed" or "ud" acceptable?**
Yes, directions are a required element. Every written prescription must contain "the directions for use of the drug" (s. 456.42(1), F.S.). For controlled substances the prescription or the pharmacist's record must also show directions for use (s. 893.04(1)(c)4, F.S.), and federal law requires directions for use on every controlled substance prescription (21 CFR 1306.05(a)). No Florida or federal text found says whether a bare "as directed" satisfies the element. Every standard found says it does not: NCPDP calls sigs that only say "as directed" or "as needed" incomplete (SCRIPT Implementation Recommendations v1.77, section 10), USP Chapter <17> says to avoid "take as directed" unless clear supplemental instructions and counseling are provided, and ISMP lists "UD" as error-prone. NCPDP adds that "take one tablet daily as directed" is acceptable because a dose and frequency are present (section 10.8.12). Benchmark reading (**inferred**): a sig that is only "ud" or "as directed", with no dose or frequency anywhere on the prescription, is a LAW-tier gap for the directions element, with STANDARD support from NCPDP, USP and ISMP. New York Schedule II and listed benzodiazepines fail a stated rule (see the state section).

**2. What may a Florida pharmacist do when directions are missing or ambiguous? What documentation is required?**
No Florida rule found says "contact the prescriber for every ambiguous sig." What exists:

- Pharmacy practice "may not be interpreted to permit an alteration of a prescriber's directions" (s. 465.003(22), F.S.).
- Interpreting prescription contents and consulting the practitioner about interpretation are tasks listed for the pharmacist or intern (r. 64B16-27.1001(1)(c)-(d)).
- Prospective drug use review must screen for incorrect dosage or duration and resolve problems, including consulting the prescriber "if necessary" (r. 64B16-27.810).
- An error or omission while receiving or verifying a prescription is a disciplinary ground (s. 465.016(1)(t), F.S.).
- For a Schedule II opioid that does not meet s. 456.44, the Board's FAQ says to contact the prescriber, and that any change "should be promptly reduced to writing and properly annotated" (Florida Board of Pharmacy FAQ).

Documentation: the patient record must hold pharmacist comments relevant to drug therapy (r. 64B16-27.800(1)(f)). No Florida rule found prescribes a format for a clarification note. The NABP Model Rules use the same "if necessary" wording as Florida's DUR rule. Benchmark reading (**inferred**): if the pharmacist would have to supply a dose or frequency, that is an alteration, so contact is required (LAW). If the sig can be read without changing the prescriber's directions (for example expanding "qd" to "daily"), the pharmacist may interpret and document (JUDGMENT, with ISMP as STANDARD context). California is stricter (see below).

**3. Schedule II (for example oxycodone): what may a pharmacist add or change after consulting the prescriber, and what may never change? Is "q4-6h prn" acceptable?**
Federal, in date order:

- **2007.** In the preamble to its multiple-prescriptions rule, DEA said the essential elements a practitioner writes on a Schedule II prescription (drug name, strength, dosage form, quantity, and the earliest fill date) "may not be modified orally" (72 FR 64921, at 64926, Nov 19, 2007). Directions for use were not named in that list.
- **2022.** DEA's current guidance says that, until DEA writes new regulations, pharmacists "should adhere to state regulations or policy" on changes to a Schedule II paper prescription after talking with the prescriber (DEA-DC-063, Oct 18, 2022).
- The 2022 DEA Pharmacist's Manual has an "Acceptable Changes to a Prescription" heading on page 38, and the section under it reads "[Reserved]". So the manual contains no list of allowed or forbidden changes.
- The often-repeated rule that the patient name, the drug and the prescriber signature may never change comes from an older DEA FAQ. I could not load the archived page this session, so that list is **not verified** from a DEA source.

Florida:

- The only explicit change authority found is s. 893.04(2)(d), F.S. After prescriber verification, the pharmacist may document the written-out quantity and the date format.
- The Board FAQ says to contact the prescriber when a Schedule II opioid prescription does not meet s. 456.44, and to annotate any change.
- No Florida rule found lists which other elements (directions, strength, dosage form) may be added after consultation, or which may never change. Benchmark reading: adding or changing Schedule II directions needs prescriber authorization and documentation (LAW, from the required element plus the bar on alteration). The exact Florida limits are not found.

PRN range:

- No federal or Florida text found forbids a frequency range.
- NCPDP says an "as needed" sig without an indication "may result in contact from the pharmacy" and recommends sending an indication (section 10.8.10).
- **Inferred**: "q4-6h prn pain" satisfies the directions element. But Florida caps a Schedule II opioid for acute pain at a 3-day supply unless the prescriber writes "ACUTE PAIN EXCEPTION" (s. 456.44(5)(a), F.S.), so days supply must be worked out from the tightest interval (q4h, up to 6 doses a day).
- Benchmark tier for the range itself is JUDGMENT. A missing strength, as in sb-045, is LAW.

**4. Which ISMP error-prone entries apply to the test sigs, and which drugs carry ISMP high-alert status?**
From the ISMP List of Error-Prone Abbreviations, Symbols, and Dose Designations (2024):

- "QD/qd" is read as qid. Write "daily". It is on the Joint Commission "Do Not Use" list.
- "OD" is listed twice: once for right eye (confused with right ear, write "right eye") and once for once daily (confused with right eye, write "daily"). So "OD" in a sig is ambiguous on ISMP's own terms.
- "U or u" for units is read as 0, 4 or cc. Write "units". It is on the Do Not Use list.
- A trailing zero ("1.0 mg") is read as 10 mg. A missing leading zero (".5 mg") is read as 5 mg. Both are on the Do Not Use list.
- "µg" is read as mg. Write "mcg".
- Also relevant: "QOD", "SSI", "UD" and "MTX".

NCPDP tells e-prescribing systems to follow the ISMP list when building sig text (section 10.8.19).

High-alert status (ISMP lists, 2021):

- Methotrexate, oral and parenteral, for non-cancer use is high-alert with special emphasis on both the community/ambulatory list and the long-term care list.
- All insulins are high-alert on both lists, with U-500 singled out.
- Digoxin, oral and parenteral, is named on the long-term care list only.
- ISMP's 2026 Targeted Medication Safety Best Practices for Hospitals keep accidental daily dosing of weekly oral methotrexate as a named issue.

Digoxin labeling (DailyMed): Lanoxin tablets come in 62.5, 125 and 250 mcg, and a current generic label lists 125 and 250 mcg. No label found expresses a tablet strength in mg. "125 mg" is 500 times the largest tablet strength, so it can only be a unit error (**inferred**).

**5. Days supply versus quantity. Is days supply required? Can quantity and days supply be used to infer frequency?**
Quantity is required. Days supply is not.

- Florida requires the quantity prescribed (s. 456.42(1), F.S.), in text and numerals for controlled substances (s. 456.42(2)).
- Federal law requires quantity prescribed (21 CFR 1306.05(a)). The NABP Model Rules require quantity but not days supply.
- No Florida, federal or NABP text found requires days supply on the prescription.
- In e-prescriptions, NCPDP carries days supply as an optional element, used mainly in prior authorization transactions (section 11.8).
- Days supply matters in Florida through limits the pharmacist must work out from the directions: the 3-day Schedule II opioid acute pain cap (s. 456.44(5)(a)), which the Board FAQ says applies to the supply dispensed, not to how long the prescription is valid.

Inferring a missing frequency from quantity and days supply is **not found** as either allowed or forbidden. **Inferred**: because s. 465.003(22) bars altering the prescriber's directions, writing in a frequency that is not on the prescription is supplying directions, so it is LAW tier (contact the prescriber). Using quantity only to confirm a frequency that is already written is JUDGMENT.

## Differs by state

Only clear differences from Florida are listed. Everything else is **not found**, not "same as Florida."

**(a) "Use as directed" or "take as directed"**

- **New York**: differs for Schedule II and the listed benzodiazepines. The rule requires "specific directions for use" with dose, frequency and maximum daily dose (10 NYCRR 80.67(b)(3)), so a bare "as directed" does not meet it (**inferred** from the text, which does not name the phrase). Non-controlled NY rules not checked.
- **California** and **Texas**: no rule found that names "as directed." Note that NCPDP says "as directed" only sigs "may not be allowed in certain states" without naming them.

**(b) Filling a missing element after consulting the prescriber**

- **California**: stricter than Florida. A pharmacist may not dispense any prescription with a significant omission, uncertainty or ambiguity and must contact the prescriber (16 CCR 1761(a)). Florida has no equivalent general rule found, only the DUR rule's "if necessary."
- **New York**: has an explicit procedure Florida lacks. The prescriber may orally supply missing information or authorize changes, a short list of elements can never be supplied or changed, and the documentation format is set (10 NYCRR 80.67(g)-(i), 80.73(k)-(m)).
- **Texas**: has explicit board guidance Florida lacks. Directions, strength, dosage form and quantity may be added or changed on a Schedule II after verbal prescriber permission. Patient, drug, prescriber and issue date never change (TSBP FAQ). The underlying rule number was not located.

**(c) Days supply or quantity rules that affect inference**

- **New York**: Schedule II and listed benzodiazepines are capped at a 30-day supply computed from the written directions (10 NYCRR 80.67(c)), so the maximum daily dose drives the allowed quantity. Florida has no general 30-day cap found, only the 3-day acute pain opioid cap.
- **California** and **Texas**: no days-supply rule found that differs from Florida.

- **Archived DEA "never change" FAQ.** The UNT web archive page returned an empty page twice. The current DEA manual section is "[Reserved]" and the 2022 guidance defers to states, so no current DEA text lists the never-change items.
- **Florida list of Schedule II changes.** I read the full list of rules in F.A.C. chapter 64B16-27 and the Board's controlled substance FAQ, and searched the Board's site. None lists allowed or forbidden changes. I searched once for a Board newsletter on this and found nothing.
- **Whether a bare "as directed" satisfies the law's directions element.** No statute, rule or agency text in Florida or federal law answers it.
- **PRN frequency ranges and inferring frequency from quantity and days supply.** No law found. Joint Commission range-order standards apply to hospitals and sit behind the Joint Commission's paid manual, so they were not read.
- **NCPDP Structured and Codified Sig Format Implementation Guide.** Member-only. The public Implementation Recommendations above cover the parts that matter here.
- **USP <17> official text.** USP-NF requires a subscription, so the text was read on a non-official mirror of USP 38. Later revisions could differ.
- **ISMP acute care high-alert list.** Not checked. The full text of r. 64B16-28.108 was not read either (search excerpt only).
